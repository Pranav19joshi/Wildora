Rishikesh

Ganga ghats, white-water rafting, riverside ashrams, and the natural gateway to Char Dham. Rafting season runs September to June.

What to do in 2–4 days

Triveni Ghat — Evening Ganga Aarti

Every evening after sunset, priests perform the aarti at Triveni Ghat with oil lamps and Sanskrit chanting. Free to attend, lasts about 45 minutes. The Parmarth Niketan ashram on the opposite bank runs a larger version. Both are worth seeing at least once.

White-water Rafting

The standard stretch is Shivpuri to Rishikesh Beach — 16 km, 2–3 hours, Grade III rapids. Longer runs from Marine Drive (24 km) and Kaudiyala (36 km) for those who want more. Season: September to June. Half a day including transport and gear. Do not book with the cheapest operator on the street.

Beatles Ashram (Chaurasi Kutia)

Maharishi Mahesh Yogi's ashram where John, Paul, George, and Ringo stayed in 1968. Now a protected heritage site, open to visitors. Overgrown meditation domes and riverside buildings — atmospheric and unhurried. Entry fee applies. Worth 2 hours.

Lakshman Jhula & Ram Jhula

Two iron suspension bridges across the Ganga. Lakshman Jhula connects the banks at Swarg Ashram. Ram Jhula is further south and less congested. Walk across, explore the ghats on both sides.

Yoga & Wellness

Yoga Niketan, Sivananda Ashram, and Parmarth Niketan all offer drop-in morning classes. Tell us if this is a priority when you enquire and we match the accommodation accordingly — riverside camps, ashram guesthouses, or proper hotels.

Rishikesh as part of a wider circuit

Almost every Char Dham route starts or ends in Haridwar, 24 km downriver. Adding 2 nights in Rishikesh before or after is easy and breaks the long mountain road journey. It also acts as a buffer day if weather delays a Himalayan road.

Corbett to Rishikesh by road is about 4–5 hours (Ramnagar → Kotdwara → Rishikesh). Works well if you have 8+ days and want wildlife followed by the river.
