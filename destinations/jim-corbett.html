Jim Corbett

India's oldest national park. Bengal tigers, sal forest, and 600+ bird species. Our office town sits at the southern gate.

How a 2–5 day visit works

Arrive at Ramnagar station or by road. Check into a lodge near the zone you have permits for. Morning safari starts at first light — around 6 am in winter, 5:30 in summer. Afternoon safari from 2:30 pm. Two safaris a day is standard. Three nights gives you four safaris with a buffer day.

We do not promise a tiger sighting. Nobody honestly can. We put you in the right zone, with the right guide, from a lodge that is not an hour from the gate.

The zones

Bijrani

The most productive zone for tiger sightings. Grassland and dense forest mix. Open October to June. Entry from Amdanda gate, close to Ramnagar. Our first recommendation for most guests.

Dhikala

Open chaurs (grasslands), elephant herds, and the best tiger habitat in the park. Entry through Dhangadi gate. Night stays in Forest Rest Houses inside the core zone are available, but they book out 45 days in advance. Needs more lead time — worth it if you plan ahead.

Jhirna

The only zone open year-round, including monsoon. Sloth bears, leopards, elephants. Less crowded than Bijrani. Good birding at the Ramganga reservoir edge.

Dhela

Open year-round. Mix of grassland and dry deciduous forest. Good for deer herds and wild boar. Lower tiger pressure than Bijrani, but a quieter, less crowded experience.

Durga Devi

North-east zone along the Ramganga valley. Best for birding — 550+ recorded species. Himalayan foothills visible on clear days. Not a primary tiger zone but worth a half-day for serious birders.

Getting to Ramnagar

By train: Ranikhet Express (15013/15014) from Old Delhi station — arrives Ramnagar in 6–7 hours, overnight. The Corbett Link Express also runs select days. Most guests prefer this over the road.

By road: Delhi → NH9 → Moradabad → Rampur → Ramnagar. About 5–6 hours depending on Moradabad traffic. We arrange Ramnagar pickup either way.
